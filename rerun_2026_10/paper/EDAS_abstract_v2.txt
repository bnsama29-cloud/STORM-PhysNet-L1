Electrons above 2 MeV at geostationary orbit (GEO) are a recurring internal-charging hazard for communication, navigation and Earth-observation satellites. We present STORM-PhysNet, a Transformer that forecasts GOES-15 >2 MeV log-flux 1, 6 and 12 h ahead from a 72 h history of flux and OMNI solar-wind/geomagnetic drivers, with a learnable propagation delay, a Bz-conditioned gate and residual-over-persistence heads. We evaluate it under a leakage-free protocol (no back-filled gaps, causal Kp, boundary-buffered windows) on three disjoint test periods (2012-13, 2015-16, 2018-20; 34 554 windows, 937 storm-time windows, Dst down to -198 nT), with ten seeds per model and paired tests against an architecture-matched Transformer, a residual-head Transformer, a flux-only Transformer and an LSTM. STORM-PhysNet reaches a block-averaged prediction efficiency of 0.977/0.865/0.803 at 1/6/12 h (persistence 0.968/0.581/0.261). It beats the matched Transformer at 1 h in every block (+0.008, p = 3.5e-18), ties it at 6 h and trails it at 12 h (-0.010). Ablations show that the 1 h gain comes from the residual-over-persistence head and the 6-12 h skill from the solar-wind inputs; the delay and gate do not improve clean-data accuracy. They do, however, coincide with much greater robustness: with unit-variance noise on the drivers STORM-PhysNet keeps 1 h PE at 0.970 (matched Transformer 0.892), and on GSAT-19 GRASP data from an Indian-longitude GEO sensor it transfers zero-shot with 12 h PE 0.738 versus 0.644 (p = 5.3e-4). After fine-tuning both models converge (0.802 vs 0.794).
